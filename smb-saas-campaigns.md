# InboxKit Campaigns: SaaS, 10–200 Employees

**Segment:** B2B SaaS companies, 10–200 employees. Usually no real SDR team, founder or first sales hire doing outbound manually, marketing spend concentrated in paid and content.

**The offer (same across all three personas):** We run outbound for you on 10–20K of your ICP and book meetings on your calendar.

**The proof:** InboxKit added $3M in ARR last quarter from outbound email alone. We built the infrastructure for ourselves first, then opened it up.

**Three campaigns, one per persona.** Never run two of these into the same company at the same time. If you have multiple contacts at one account, sequence them: Head of Sales first, then CEO 30 days later if no reply.

---

## Why the case study works on this segment

At this size the buyer's real question is not "does cold email work." It is "does it work for a company like mine, run by people who actually do it." A vendor saying "we grew our own company $3M last quarter on the exact thing we're selling you" answers that in one line. Lead with it, don't bury it.

Three ways to phrase it, in decreasing aggression:

- "We added $3M in ARR last quarter from cold email alone. Same infrastructure we'd run for you."
- "Last quarter cold email put $3M of new ARR on our books. It's the only channel we run."
- "We're not selling you a theory. Outbound email added $3M ARR to our own business last quarter."

Use the first for CEOs, the second for Heads of Sales, the third for Heads of Marketing.

# CAMPAIGN 1 — HEAD OF SALES / VP SALES

**What they care about:** Hitting the number. Their reps are not generating enough top of funnel. They are personally in deals they should not be in. Every meeting on the calendar is worth something concrete to them.

**Tone:** Peer to peer. Direct. Numbers.

Subject: `meetings for {{company}}`

> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> I'm {{sender_name}} from InboxKit. We run outbound email for B2B SaaS companies — we build the sending infrastructure, write the sequences, send to your ICP, and book the replies onto your team's calendar.
>
> Worth knowing before you decide if this is credible: cold email added $3M in ARR to our own business last quarter. It's the only channel we run.
>
> The offer for {{company}}: we run 10–20K of your ICP and book you meetings. You don't touch it.
>
> Worth 20 minutes?
>
> {{sender_name}}

>
> Week 1 we agree the ICP and build the list. Weeks 2–3 we warm infrastructure and write sequences. Week 4 we start sending. Meetings usually start landing 10–14 days after first send.
>

### Email 4 — Day 14 (same thread)

### Email 5 — Day 19 (breakup)
Subject: `closing the loop`

> Assuming the timing's off, {{first_name}}.

> Standing offer whenever it's useful: we run 10–20K of your ICP, you get the meetings. Same play that added $3M ARR to our own business last quarter.
>
> {{sender_name}}

| CEO | `outbound for {{company}}`, `quick one`, `$3M last quarter` |

## Rules for this segment

- **Under 90 words on email one.** These people read on a phone between meetings.
- **No links until email three.** A link in a cold email one is a deliverability tax you don't need.
- **Ask a real question, not "are you interested."** The ICP sizing ask in campaign 2 and 3 converts better than a meeting ask because it costs the prospect one sentence.
- **One persona per account at a time.** Running all three into a 40-person company reads as spam and burns the account.
- **Sequence multi-contact accounts.** Head of Sales first. If no reply in 30 days, CEO. Marketing last.
- **Suppress on reply anywhere in the account.** If the CEO replies, stop the sales sequence immediately.

## Variables to enrich

| Variable | Source |
|---|---|
| `first_name`, `title`, `company`, `domain` | Standard |

## Suggested launch

Split the first send three ways by persona, 3–5K contacts each. Review reply rate and positive reply rate at day 14. The CEO campaign will likely win on reply rate and lose on call quality; judge on booked meetings, not replies.
